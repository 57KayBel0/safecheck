SafeCheck privacy policy

Draft updated 2 November 2026. SafeCheck is a personal safety prototype created by Kabelo Motshabi Makgae for check-ins and trusted-contact alerts.

SafeCheck is operated by Kabelo Motshabi Makgae. For support, feedback or privacy requests, email kabelo.6a8fbeb6546d3829dedadcf7@gmail.com.

Draft for owner review before publication. Backup and log retention, current live-delivery configuration and the process for handling data requests still need review.

Account and Google identity data

Email accounts use an email address, password-based authentication and verification/recovery flows through Serverpod authentication. Passwords are handled by that authentication system, not included in contact alerts.

When Google sign-in is configured and you choose it, Google provides an account identifier, email address and its verification status, name and, where available, a profile picture URL. The Serverpod Google identity provider records the Google account identifier and email, and can create an authentication profile with your name and download your profile picture to its configured storage. Google identity data is used to authenticate you and establish your account. The requested scopes are openid, userinfo.email and userinfo.profile. SafeCheck does not request Google Contacts, Gmail or Drive access. Google authorization codes and tokens are processed during sign-in; the OAuth client credential stays on the server.

The app normally holds its SafeCheck login credentials in memory. When you select Remember me, it uses the SDK's device/browser storage to persist a session; an email entered through email login can also be remembered. Turning Remember me off removes the stored session and remembered email. Signing out clears stored session credentials but does not delete server records; a remembered email can remain until Remember me is turned off.

Profiles, contacts and trips

Your SafeCheck profile records your chosen display name and South African phone number, account identifier and creation/update times. Trusted-contact records include names, phone numbers, optional relationships, notification preferences, invite links and acceptance/decline status and times.

Phone contact selection reads the contact you choose on a supported device or browser. You review its name and number before saving; manual entry is available. SafeCheck does not import your entire address book.

Trips record their type, description, duration, start/deadline/end times, check-in status, location-sharing choice and a snapshot of the contacts at trip start. Alerts and message history record recipients, message content, channel, timestamps, delivery/simulation status and failure details.

Optional AI trip planning

If you choose Describe with AI, your typed trip description and the prior exchanges in that planning conversation are sent through SafeCheck's server to Meta Model API. A server time reference in UTC and South African time is included. SafeCheck does not automatically add your profile, trusted-contact records or recorded GPS points. Any personal information you type yourself will be included. Meta processes these requests under its API terms and privacy practices.

The planning conversation is held in the app's memory for follow-up questions. SafeCheck's planning service does not save prompts or draft responses to its database. It saves per-account request counts and time windows to enforce usage limits. After you explicitly confirm a reviewed draft, the resulting trip details are stored like a manually created trip. AI planning can make mistakes; review the description and exact check-in time before confirming. Manual setup remains available.

Location and tracking links

Location sharing is selected per trip: off, live, or visible after a missed check-in. When enabled and device permission is granted, the running app requests GPS fixes approximately every 30 seconds. Latitude, longitude, reported accuracy, fix time and server receipt time are stored. Updates depend on the device, browser, app activity and network; continuous background tracking is not guaranteed. Location is not collected for trips whose location-sharing mode is off.

Per-contact tracking links reveal trip status, traveller name and, when permitted by the trip's sharing mode, the stored location route. Possession of a link is sufficient to access its page without an account. Anyone to whom a link is forwarded may use it. Live mode shows locations while the trip is active; after-alert mode shows them after a missed check-in. A safe check-in hides location. After a missed check-in, location visibility ends after 48 hours. This is an access rule, not deletion of the records or link.

Use and sharing

Data supports authentication, profile/contact management, deadlines, check-ins, alerts, trip history and chosen location sharing. Contact-facing invite pages disclose the contact and traveller names and invite status. Invites are shared by the traveller; SafeCheck does not automatically send them. Acceptance/decline can be changed using the invite link. These choices are not a general account deletion or all-channel opt-out mechanism.

Alert content may disclose your display name, phone number, trip type or description, timing and missed check-in, and a tracking link when enabled. Trip snapshots determine alert recipients; later contact edits do not rewrite those snapshots. SMS preferences can permit SMS even without an accepted WhatsApp invitation.

The default sender simulates messages. Simulated SMS is labelled exactly “Demo — SMS not sent”; WhatsApp is simulated too. An app alert or simulated message does not establish notification of a contact. The code also supports real SMS through BulkSMS only when live mode and both server-side provider credentials are explicitly configured. In that mode recipient phone numbers and SMS content are submitted to BulkSMS and may reach the recipient. WhatsApp remains simulated by the implemented sender. This draft does not assert that real delivery is enabled on the deployed service.

Serverpod Cloud hosts the production service and its database/storage. Serverpod Cloud's email service handles production verification/recovery email. Google processes Google sign-in. Maps fetch tiles from OpenStreetMap; public tracking pages also load Leaflet assets from cdnjs.cloudflare.com. Those requests expose normal connection information, such as IP address and requested map area, to the relevant provider. Choosing Open in Google Maps sends the shown coordinates to Google Maps. Providers have their own privacy practices.

Storage, retention and deletion

Account, profile, contact, trip, alert, message and location records are stored in the server database; authentication images use configured cloud storage or the database fallback. Server/hosting logs may contain operation metadata, errors and phone numbers or message information logged by the implementation. The Serverpod Cloud project region is Europe. Public and private object-storage regions are Europe. These facts do not establish the location of every provider or backup. Log/backup lifetimes and provider retention periods still need confirmation from the operator.

The recurring server sweep deletes location points whose server receipt time is more than 30 days old when the sweep runs successfully. This does not delete trips, snapshots, alerts, messages or tracking-link records and does not establish backup deletion. Cleanup depends on the running server.

Removing a contact deletes that current contact row. It does not remove existing trip snapshots, alerts, messages or tracking links. Profile editing updates the current profile. There is no implemented user-facing account deletion or complete history-erasure workflow, and no automatic retention period is implemented for these other application records. Signing out, revoking Google access or a tracking link ceasing to show locations does not delete SafeCheck records.
